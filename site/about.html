About the project

Why this logbook?

From 24 October to 1 November 2026 I joined the China Grand Challenge 2026, a hyperSPRINT Grand Challenge organised by iteratec: an invitation-only European delegation that went to see how humanoid robotics and embodied AI can solve real operational needs in Europe – and why Chinese teams build and deploy so fast. Our route led through Beijing, Zoucheng (Shandong), Shanghai and Shenzhen.

We saw Galbot in Beijing (the G1 humanoid, with one general "big brain" model across robots), ROKAE's factory in Zoucheng (industrial arms and the Helios wheeled dual-arm humanoid), AgiBot in Shanghai (a facility tour plus AgiBot Innovation Day 2026), a robotics training-data centre in Shanghai, Linkerbot in Shenzhen (dexterous hands and teleoperation) and AI² Robotics in Shenzhen (a data-collection centre with teleoperation and an edge VLA model).

My takeaways: the hardware is already good enough – these robots dance and perform. The software still mostly runs fixed routines. Real autonomy needs the "big brain", a world model that is still in development. First deployments are demos and pilots in industry, entertainment and guidance, and the ROI is not proven yet – which is exactly where Europe can come in. My mental model: a humanoid is a body, plus a small brain (real-time motion, balance, force, grasping), plus a big brain (AI that understands, plans and learns).

I'm originally a software and AI guy, and the trip made me want to understand robotics with my own hands. So I started playing with a robot arm, the Seeed reBot Arm B601-RS: teleoperation, calibration, recording demonstrations, training small policies like ACT. This logbook shares that journey, mistakes included, so you can replicate it.
