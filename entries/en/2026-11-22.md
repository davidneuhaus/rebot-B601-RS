A night session straight after Day 3: the arm kept dropping, so we first hunted for the reasons – and ended with the calibration we had been chasing for two days. All arm motions were David's own runs.

## What we did

- **v3.9.6 – the 00:23 run dropped at pose 7.** Not a cable this time: the shared USB 2.0 hub stalled for about a second, and both cameras and all 7 motor feeds froze at once (8 GB of RAM swapping, GUI at about 200 % CPU). Our own rule "feedback frozen for 1 s → torque off" then cut the motors. Now we **keep the torque and re-send the last target for up to 3 s** while polling.
- **v3.9.7 – the 00:33 run sagged.** The shoulder-lift driver heated from 32 to 78 °C holding about 13 Nm and threw fault 0x12 (overcurrent + HALL), so the joint went limp. Now any motor fault turns all motors off. A temperature watch warns at 60 °C, pauses at the start pose from 64 °C until below 52 °C and soft-stops at 70 °C. On camera loss the arm waits at the start pose and retries. The sync script also scrubs private IPs, paths and asset tags before pushing. Code: [commit 95f2dc4](https://github.com/davidneuhaus/rebot-B601-RS/commit/95f2dc4); later changes go into v3.9.8 together with an English UI.
- **The camera swap, in detail.** The Logitech C270 runs at about 30 fps at 640×480; at 1280×720 uncompressed it manages only 0–1.5 fps on the shared hub. Fixed focus, sharp from about 30 cm. The gripper jaw hid the lower part of the image until David tilted the camera up.

![The C270 wrist view of the board: sharp, but the board edge fills the frame.](day4-c270-wrist.webp)

## Mindset

Our calibration planner assumed the **old Innomaker geometry** (camera looking along the fingers) and aimed the fingers at the board – so the C270, mounted differently, saw only the board's edge. The lesson we took: don't plan from an idealised model of the camera mount. **Start from a pose that already sees the target** and wiggle around it.

## Wins

- **02:48 – 13 of 20 poses good, applied.** Wrist intrinsics 0.463 px RMS, full-chain reprojection 1.56 px, hand-eye AX=XB median 0.64° / 3.7 mm, board-pose spread 1.7 mm RMS, camera-to-gripper offset [2.9, −8.3, 26.3] mm. The overview camera sits at 0.43 px.
- **The "jitter" planner works:** 20 small joint offsets around a taught pose that sees the board, offsets halved after the first failures. David re-taught the pose at 02:47.

![The jitter plan in the robot base frame: black is the taught pose, the other poses fan out around it.](day4-handeye-plan.webp)

## Overnight build and a quiet morning (03:05–08:14)

After 03:05 there was no arm motion. In about 35 minutes (roughly 0.6 h) the Humanoid Project bot built `grasp.sh "grasp blue cube"` – a colour-blob cube detector, a planner and an executor – plus the new teleop UI 3.10 as a separate app. Both passed in simulation. Then the project slept until 08:14.

![The blue cube on the board, seen by the overview camera.](day4-blue-cube-overview.webp)

## Morning: the first real grasp (08:14–11:41, about 3.5 h)

- **08:20:** the dry run (`--check`) found the 51 mm blue cube (confidence 0.63) and planned 17 steps in about 2 minutes. The simulated grasp ran all 17 moves in about 106 s.
- **09:01 – first real grasp SUCCESS!** The gripper closed on the blue cube at 165.9°, kept holding through the lift, then released and returned to rest. It was far from pretty: the jaws grabbed across an edge (wrong cube yaw from the overview camera, `wrist_roll` about 51°), the motion was jerky (a full stop at all 17 waypoints at 1.5–6°/s) and the gripper opening stalls at 226°.
- **09:07:** we approved fixes: a continuous trajectory, faster speeds and a yaw check with the wrist camera.

![A morning grasp run: the gripper lifts the blue cube off the board (overview camera, cropped).](day4-first-grasp-lift.webp)

## Fails

- **Two drops** (see above) – the first from a USB stall plus our own over-eager safety rule, the second from an overheated joint.
- **Four calibration runs (02:21–02:43)** found too few board corners. Causes: wrong mount geometry in the planner, the jaw occluding part of the image, and small frames. At 02:41 only 1 of 20 poses was good.
- **A silent bug:** calibration frames were downscaled to 480×360 (the shared-frame size) instead of 640×480. Fixed.
- The GUI also needed `ulimit -n 4096` ("too many open files").
- **09:32 – the watchdog cut the torque and the arm dropped onto the board.** The fix is a soft lower before torque-off (about 1 s, at most 10°/s, then motors off; live at 10:13). The drop knocked the wrist-camera mount about 23° / 50 mm out of place and nudged the overview camera, so every target was about 70 mm off.
- **10:37 / 10:44:** two aborts ("wrist camera: object 40/63 mm from plan") traced back to the shifted cameras. We disabled the wrist check for now, added an overview drift check (planning refuses if the board moved more than 12 px) and a Recalibrate button (0.39 px reprojection).
- **11:08:** a 1-point touch correction (dx −59.8, dy +8.7 mm) cut the error from about 60 mm to 2–3 cm. A 2-point fit at 11:18 was bad (6.67° rotation, ±10 mm residuals) and we reverted it. New tools: a touch-calibration script, a wrist check and a Touch-calibrate card in the GUI (staged, not live).
- **11:27 / 11:30:** the position was right, but the jaws went for the cube's corners (wrong yaw). Re-enabling the wrist yaw check failed: the cube filled the wrist frame, got clipped at the border and the planner fell back to its planned yaw.
- One warning remains from the night: the overview table calibration needs a refresh.

## More wins

- **11:25 – hand-eye auto calibration OK again:** 7 of 7 views, chain reprojection 0.91 px, AX=XB translation median 14.3 mm (max 22 mm). We had to teach a target pose first, because the board was only partly visible (9 of 24 corners).
- **11:30 – the safety rule works:** the `shoulder_lift` driver hit 70 °C (60 °C at 11:30:23) and raised MotorHot – and the arm retreated safely instead of sagging.

## Rethink (11:32–11:41)

The hand-coded grasp depends on a fragile calibration chain, and taped camera mounts break it after every bump. So we decided:

1. **Mount the cameras rigidly first.** 3D-printed Innomaker wrist mounts (ordered on 15.11 through Craftcloud) arrive between 23 and 27.11 – to be used with a short, strain-relieved USB cable – and the overview camera gets clamped.
2. **No depth camera for now.** Too expensive (Orbbec Gemini 305 about €289, RealSense D405 about €489), and RealSense is reported to be unstable on macOS in the LeRobot docs.
3. **Next direction: record about 40 teleop demos and fine-tune SmolVLA** instead of sinking more time into hand-coded calibration.

## Learnings

- **Calibration planners must model the real camera mount** – or, better, plan around a taught pose that already sees the target.
- **Check the whole image pipeline end to end.** A hidden downscale cost us hours.
- **Watch motor temperatures on long holds.** 13 Nm held for minutes cooks a driver; a safety rule that retreats instead of cutting power saved the arm.
- **Soft-lower before torque-off.** A hard stop drops the arm – and the drop wrecked the calibration.
- **Rigid mounts beat clever software.** Taped cameras drift with every bump, and every drift costs a recalibration.
- ACT training needs **new demos with the C270**, because the old demos use the old camera.
